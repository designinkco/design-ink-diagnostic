01

Executive Summary

The headline finding.

The highest-ranking interior design websites are not the most famous ones. World-class design studios like Flack, Amber Interiors and Tamsin Johnson rely on Instagram, press and referrals to drive business. When judged on whether a cold visitor lands and is moved to inquire, they perform poorly.

The sites that win the balanced scorecard treat their website as a sales asset, not a portfolio. They invest in SEO, conversion path and trust signals just as much as in visual design.

For a growth consultancy that helps interior designers get customers, this is the most important lesson in the entire dataset.

"We build interior design websites that win clients, not awards."

Positioning thesis — Design Ink Co.

02

Methodology

The judging matrix.

Each site was scored out of 100, with four pillars weighted equally at 25 points each. Every homepage was scraped via Firecrawl with structured data extraction across two dozen indicators per site.

25 pts

SEO & Discoverability

Title tag quality, meta description, content depth via blog or journal, nav structure, on-page signals, location and service pages.

25 pts

Design & UX

Visual hierarchy, typography, imagery quality, navigation clarity, brand point of view, performance and loading.

25 pts

Conversion

Above-fold CTA, inquiry form quality, multiple CTAs throughout, services articulation, lead capture, friction in inquiry path.

25 pts

Trust & Authority

Testimonials, press mentions, awards, portfolio depth, about-page substance, process transparency.

08

The Insight

The 7 patterns that separate the top 5 from the bottom 5.

Every top-5 site does most of these. No bottom-5 site does more than two. This is the playbook.

  1. They write title tags as if they want to be found.

    Free-and-foundational SEO move that the bottom 5 simply do not do. A first-time visitor lands from search, and the page title is the first signal of legitimacy.

    Top 5: "Luxury Interior Design London | Studio Augustine" / "Interior Design London | Residential & Commercial Interior Designers | Oraanj"

    Bottom 5: "Home | Amber Interiors" / "Home - Heidi Caillier" / "Home | Arent&Pyke"

  2. They have a clear, repeated, above-the-fold CTA.

    The visitor should not have to hunt for the next step. The top 5 average 3-5 visible CTAs per homepage. The bottom 5 average less than one.

    Top 5: "Book a Design Consult" (Born & Bred) / "Book a Discovery Call" (Studiomint) / "Discuss Your Project" (Oraanj) / "Inquire" (Studio McGee)

    Bottom 5: "Subscribe" or nothing.

  3. They publish content beyond their portfolio.

    Journals, blogs and media libraries drive organic traffic and return visits. No content equals no organic traffic equals total dependence on PR and Instagram.

    Studio McGee runs Read, Watch and Recipe sections. Katharine Pooley has a journal. Born & Bred publishes consultation diaries. Oraanj has 180 case studies. Studiomint has articles. Bottom 5 mostly publish nothing.

  4. They stack trust signals.

    Testimonials + press + awards + portfolio depth + about substance. Trust isn't a single asset, it's a layered stack. Top 5 average 5+ trust signals on the homepage. Bottom 5 average 1-2.

  5. They articulate services, and ideally price ranges.

    Born & Bred has packages. Oraanj has £600 / £900 / £1,500 tiers. Studiomint has 7 sector pages. Studio McGee has Full-Service vs Virtual. A visitor who can't tell what you sell will not inquire.

  6. They show the human behind the brand.

    Katharine's photo and bio. Lauren Li at Sisalla. Shea McGee front-and-centre. Brian Brown's manifesto. Top sites lean into personal authority. Bottom 5 hide behind imagery.

  7. They have a brand point of view expressed in words, not just images.

    "New Heritage" (Studio McGee). "British Interior Designer of the Decade" (Katharine). "Scandinavian soul with Australian ease" (Sisalla). Bottom 5 let their images do all the talking, and images alone do not communicate positioning.

09

The Build

The 10-component growth website blueprint.

The synthesised blueprint based on what the top 5 sites do. Build every new interior design website against this checklist. Use it as a diagnostic for prospects and as the spec for your own consultancy site.

  1. SEO-optimised title with service + location + brand. e.g. "Luxury Interior Design London | [Studio Name]"
  2. Above-the-fold CTA ("Book a consultation" with Calendly or equivalent), repeated 3+ times on the homepage.
  3. Services page with tiered packages. Price-anchored is ideal but not required. The visitor should know what you sell and at what level.
  4. 3+ named testimonials with photo or location attached. Real names beat generic praise every time.
  5. Press logo strip (House & Garden, Vogue Living, AD, local publications, anything credible the studio has been featured in).
  6. Awards strip or industry-body badges (SBID, BIID, equivalent). Visual proof of legitimacy.
  7. Founder photo and story on the about page, surfaced on the homepage. The human element is the trust accelerator.
  8. Branded design point of view in words, not just images. A one-line positioning statement that anchors everything.
  9. A journal or blog with at least quarterly posts. Content gravity drives organic traffic and creates reasons to return.
  10. Trust-stacking footer with social proof, accreditations and registered company info. The closing argument of every page.

What To Do With This

Three moves you can make today.

The research is only useful if it changes what you do next. Here are the most concrete starting points.

  1. Score your site. The 10-Point Diagnostic is the same balanced-scorecard methodology condensed into four minutes. Instant result. Take it now.
  2. Pick your anchor reference. Born & Bred Studio is the most replicable template for a boutique practice (Squarespace, Calendly, journal, niche positioning). Studio McGee is the aspirational stretch goal.
  3. Start with the lowest-effort win. Rewrite your homepage title tag as service + location + studio name. The single highest-impact, lowest-effort change in the entire blueprint.

Honest caveats

The scorecard rewards conversion-oriented sites. If you ever need a "best for design" or "best for SEO" cut alone, the ranking would re-sort. Heidi Caillier returned a thin scrape because her site is heavily JavaScript-rendered; the score reflects what a search engine or first-time visitor would also experience, so it stays in scope, but flagging.
